Executive Summary
Big Ten Women's Gymnastics Revenue Share

Ask Case Path Risks

1% for the Future

Invest Where Future Audience Growth Already Exists

Big Ten women's gymnastics is a high-engagement, sponsor-safe, digitally native sport with Olympic familiarity and fast-rising fan momentum. The conference can lead women's athletics by making a modest, defendable allocation now.

One percent is not a threat to football. It is a strategic investment in the future value of the Big Ten.

1%

Guaranteed allocation of institutional revenue-sharing pools to women's gymnastics

  • Financially modest enough to be politically survivable.
  • Large enough to materially strengthen program sustainability and athlete support.
  • Aligned with the Big Ten's stated leadership in women's athletics.
  • Built as a pilot model for future Olympic-sport investment.

The formal ask

A clear allocation, not an open-ended demand.

The proposal should avoid parity language and lead with strategic institutional value. The ask is intentionally specific, moderate, and structured for administrators, donors, and football stakeholders to defend.

1% Small enough to protect football priorities. Meaningful enough to change the future of the sport.

  1. Guaranteed revenue-share allocation

    Allocate 1% of institutional revenue-sharing pools to women's gymnastics programs and athletes.

  2. NIL and branding infrastructure

    Support gymnasts with conference-level tools that help athletes build visibility and commercial value.

  3. Compensation-table representation

    Include women's gymnastics in future athlete compensation committees and planning discussions.

  4. Annual investment transparency

    Report annually on women's sports investment so progress is visible, measurable, and credible.

The business case

This is not charity. It is future-facing portfolio strategy.

Women's gymnastics gives the Big Ten a rare combination: elite competition, family-friendly audience appeal, social-first content, recognizable athletes, and an investment level that is modest relative to conference economics.

Audience momentum

Women's sports are expanding quickly, and gymnastics is positioned to benefit from rising championship viewership, sponsor demand, and athlete-driven social engagement.

Digital product fit

Routines are short-form friendly, visually dynamic, easy to package, and natural fuel for highlights, athlete storytelling, and younger fan acquisition.

Institutional value

The sport strengthens university brand, enrollment influence, donor diversification, local engagement, and visible leadership in women's athletics.

Brand-safe sponsorship

Gymnastics offers family audience appeal, sponsor comfort, and a positive public narrative without asking partners to absorb unnecessary controversy.

Retention and support

Targeted revenue-share investment can improve athlete support, program stability, and talent retention without materially affecting football competitiveness.

Olympic familiarity

The public already understands the sport. Olympic relevance gives collegiate gymnastics a recognition advantage few Olympic sports can match.

Campaign structure

The campaign works only if the conference voice is unified.

Individual programs can be isolated. A coordinated Big Ten coalition is harder to dismiss and easier to frame as responsible conference leadership.

Build the coalition

Unite coaches, athletes, alumni, and supporters around shared language, shared financial framing, and shared priorities.

Prove the value

Compile attendance, streaming, social engagement, merchandise, alumni, recruiting, and local economic data by institution.

Shape the narrative

Lead with growth, sustainability, opportunity, and conference leadership. Avoid attacking football or framing the ask as anti-men's sports.

Humanize the athletes

Show academic excellence, community presence, leadership development, workload, and athlete stories that make the investment real.

Risk controls

The biggest risks are manageable if the frame stays disciplined.

The campaign should stay calm, data-rich, and future-oriented. It cannot become a fight with football, a vague equality argument, or a school-by-school lobbying effort.

Football resistance

Counter by emphasizing the allocation is financially negligible relative to football economics and valuable for Big Ten public leadership.

Non-revenue sport bias

Shift the conversation from current profit to audience growth, brand lift, media value, enrollment influence, and donor diversification.

Olympic-sport infighting

Position gymnastics as a pilot and proof-of-concept, not an exclusive entitlement or a permanent exception.

Title IX complexity

Frame the proposal as strategic allocation, conference-supported athlete investment, and good-faith gender-equity progress. Legal review should precede external use.

Public backlash

Prepare messages around athlete workload, entertainment value, conference visibility, and modern college athletics realities.

Fragmented leadership

Do not let institutions advocate separately. A unified conference-wide voice is the leverage point.

Bottom line

The Big Ten can lead rather than react.

The future value of college athletics will not come only from preserving old revenue structures. It will come from investing early in the sports, athletes, and audiences positioned for future growth.

Women's gymnastics is one of the clearest opportunities. One percent is not symbolic. It is a strategic investment in the future of the conference.
